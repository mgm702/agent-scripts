# Find Community

**Start with community, not with a product idea.** The best minimalist businesses come from people already embedded in a community who notice a problem worth solving. You don't "find" a community — you already belong to several.

## When to Use

- Someone wants to start a business but has no specific idea yet
- Trying to choose a niche or audience to serve
- Wondering where to begin as an entrepreneur

## Identify Your Communities

Walk the user through:
1. **What communities are you already part of?** Professional groups, hobbies, online forums, alumni, identity-based groups, parent groups, religious communities.
2. **Where do you spend time online?** Reddit, Discord, Slack, X, Substacks, YouTube, Facebook groups.
3. **What problems do you hear complained about repeatedly?** The best ideas come from persistent, recurring pain in communities you understand deeply.
4. **Which would you be excited to serve for years?** This isn't a weekend project.

## Evaluation Criteria

For each candidate community, assess:
- **Genuine membership** — do you understand its language, values, culture? Are you contributing, not just lurking?
- **Painful enough to pay for** — would people exchange money for a solution? Not every problem is a business.
- **Reachable** — do you know where they gather? Can you contact them directly?
- **Right size** — large enough to sustain a business, small enough that you can dominate the niche.

## Anti-patterns

- Inventing a community from scratch instead of joining an existing one
- Choosing a community purely for market size rather than genuine interest
- Skipping participation and jumping straight to "what can I sell"
- Targeting too broad an audience ("everyone who uses the internet")

## Output

Narrow to 1–3 communities the user could realistically serve. For each, note:
- The community
- The persistent problem
- How the user is connected to it
- Where it gathers (online and offline)

Hand off the most promising problem to the `validate-idea` skill to test demand before building.
